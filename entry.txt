Wednesday, 11 November 2026, was dominated by war, diplomacy and climate. The Iran war entered its ninth month: US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris that Washington's economic campaign against Tehran was devastating enough that no major combat operations were needed, while Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran was still waiting for a US answer to its seven conditions for talks and that the Strait of Hormuz would stay closed until they were met. Iran said three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines, and monitors counted 37 ships attacked in October — the highest monthly total since the war began — with at least 10 more hit in November. In Washington, the Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots in the US and Europe, including one at RAF Fairford in England, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after threats to his life. British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia, and NATO jets scrambled after drones strayed into Baltic airspace. In Abu Dhabi, the first trilateral talks between Ukraine, Russia and the United States ended with agreement on a large prisoner exchange but no peace breakthrough. The House voted 211-204 to send the impeachment resolution against Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it. At COP31 in Antalya, UN chief Antonio Guterres warned leaders that the slow response to climate change had put the world on a path to destruction. Spain held its first general strike in 14 years over housing costs, Israel swore in a new parliament with no government in sight, and Kenya discharged its last Ebola patient, starting a 42-day countdown to being declared Ebola-free.
